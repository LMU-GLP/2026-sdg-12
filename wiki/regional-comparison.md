# Comparing Los Angeles, South Korea, and Southeast Asia

A caution before the numbers: these three regions are not measured the same
way in our sources. LA/California figures are landfill-disposal tonnage under
a state law; Korea's are annual discard-and-recycling estimates from a single
article; Southeast Asia's are a mix of one country's daily-waste estimate
(Malaysia) and another's import-enforcement numbers (Indonesia). None of
these three numbers should be read as directly comparable, per the same rule
that applies to the global emissions figures elsewhere in this wiki — different
measurement, different year, different thing being counted.

| Region | What we actually have a figure for | Governance response |
|---|---|---|
| Los Angeles / California | **1.2 million tons of textiles disposed of in 2021**, 95% of it reusable or recyclable [California SB 707](../sources/la-sb707-textile-recovery.md) | SB 707 (2024): first US textile EPR law, but no household-facing change required before **2030** [California SB 707](../sources/la-sb707-textile-recovery.md) |
| South Korea | **800,000 tonnes of clothing/textiles discarded annually**, only **12% recycled locally** [Korea fast fashion boom](../sources/korea-fast-fashion-boom.md) | No government system for textiles — **80%** handled by unregulated private exporters [Korea fast fashion boom](../sources/korea-fast-fashion-boom.md) |
| Southeast Asia | Malaysia: **~2,000 tonnes of textile waste per day** [Kloth Cares](../sources/sea-kloth-cares-circular-fashion.md); Indonesia: **7,000+ bundles** ($5.3B) of illegal secondhand imports destroyed in one 2023 raid [Indonesia crackdown](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md) | No single regional policy — grassroots programs (Kloth Cares, Style Theory, moreloop) and Indonesia's unilateral import ban, which conflicts with the region also being a *destination* for used clothing [Kloth Cares](../sources/sea-kloth-cares-circular-fashion.md), [Indonesia crackdown](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md) |

## What's genuinely comparable

One pattern holds across all three, even though the raw tonnage figures
don't: **in every region, the private sector or civil society is doing more
of the actual diversion work than government regulation is**, right now.

- LA/California: SB 707 exists on paper, but its own household-facing
  requirements don't start until 2030
  [California SB 707](../sources/la-sb707-textile-recovery.md).
- Korea: 80% of clothing-waste handling is private-sector, by the source's
  own account
  [Korea fast fashion boom](../sources/korea-fast-fashion-boom.md).
- Southeast Asia: the working examples in this wiki (Kloth Cares, Style
  Theory, moreloop) are NGOs and startups, not state programs, and the one
  state-led action documented here (Indonesia's import ban) is aimed at
  protecting domestic manufacturing, not diverting waste
  [Kloth Cares](../sources/sea-kloth-cares-circular-fashion.md),
  [Indonesia crackdown](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md).

## What's different

- Only Korea has a **proven, government-run mandatory model** for *any*
  household waste stream — it's just for food, not textiles
  [Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md). Neither
  LA nor Southeast Asia has anything structurally similar for either waste
  stream in our sources.
- Southeast Asia is the only region in this comparison that is documented as
  a **net importer/destination** for other countries' used clothing, which
  changes the shape of the problem: it's not only "our household throws
  clothes away," it's "our country receives clothes other households threw
  away"
  [Indonesia crackdown](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md).

## Gaps

We do not have a single figure measuring the same thing (e.g., "tons of
textile waste per capita per year") across all three regions. Anyone using
this page for a pitch should say that directly rather than implying the
numbers above are apples-to-apples.
