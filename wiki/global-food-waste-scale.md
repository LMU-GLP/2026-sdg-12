# How much food waste is a household problem, globally

Source: [UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024](../sources/unep-food-waste-index-2024.md)

In 2022, the world wasted **"1.05 billion metric tons (1.16 billion tons) of
food — amounting to one-fifth (19%) of food available to consumers"**
([UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024](../sources/unep-food-waste-index-2024.md)).

Of that total, households — not restaurants, not grocery stores — were the
largest source:

- Households: **"631 million metric tons, equivalent to 60%"**
- Food service and retail combined: **"421 million metric tons, or 40% (290
  and 131 million metric tons, respectively)"**

On a per-person basis: **"each person wastes 79kg of food annually"**, on
average, at the household level
([UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024](../sources/unep-food-waste-index-2024.md)).

## Why this matters for the challenge

Our challenge asks how to help a household buy, use, and throw away smarter.
This source is the reason that's the right level to intervene at: 60% of all
food wasted worldwide happens after it reaches a household, not before.

## Climate cost

**"Food loss and waste generates 8% to 10% of global greenhouse gas (GHG)
emissions — almost five times the total emissions from the aviation
sector"** ([UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024](../sources/unep-food-waste-index-2024.md)).
This lines up with the [South Korea](south-korea.md) source's separate claim
that biodegrading food waste alone is 6% of global emissions — the two
figures come from different sources measuring slightly different things
(all food loss and waste, vs. biodegradation specifically) and are kept
separate here rather than combined.

## A regional data point inside the same report

The report cites a U.S. example: the Pacific Coast Food Waste Commitment
reduced **"190,000 tons of food valued at $311 million"** from 2019–2022.
No comparable dollar or tonnage figure exists in our California-specific
source ([LA / SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)), so this
number should not be assumed to describe California specifically — the
Pacific Coast Food Waste Commitment is a separate, multi-state initiative.

## A caution on comparing this to Korea's figure

South Korea's source in this wiki states Koreans generate **130 kg of food
waste per person per year**. That is not the same measurement as this
report's 79 kg global household average — different methodologies, different
scopes, possibly different definitions of "food waste." See
[Los Angeles vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md) for why these two
numbers can't be subtracted or ranked against each other.
