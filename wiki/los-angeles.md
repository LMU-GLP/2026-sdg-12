# Los Angeles / California

## Fast fashion and textile waste

- **In 2021, about 1.2 million tons of textiles were disposed of in
  California**, despite **95% of that material being reusable or
  recyclable**, per the office of state Senator Josh Newman
  [California SB 707](../sources/la-sb707-textile-recovery.md).
- California passed **SB 707, the Responsible Textile Recovery Act**, in
  October 2024 — described as **"the nation's first extended producer
  responsibility program for textiles."** It requires apparel and textile
  producers to join a Producer Responsibility Organization (PRO), approved by
  CalRecycle, covering collection, sorting, repair, and recycling statewide
  [California SB 707](../sources/la-sb707-textile-recovery.md).
- The law's own timeline is slow: PRO approval isn't due until **March 1,
  2026**, program implementation until **July 1, 2028**, and the first
  collection/recycling plan not until **July 1, 2030**
  [California SB 707](../sources/la-sb707-textile-recovery.md). Nothing about
  how an LA household disposes of clothing changes under this law before
  then.

## Behavioral precedent: organic waste

California's clothing law is structurally similar to a program it already
runs for a different waste stream. **SB 1383** set organic-waste diversion
targets of **50% below 2014 levels by 2020 and 75% by 2025**, with state
enforcement starting January 2022
[California SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md). That program is
badly behind: ReFED's tracker shows **over 300,000 more tons of food waste
went to California landfills in 2022 than in the year SB 1383 was enacted**,
and CalRecycle's own director says "I don't know when we will reach our 75
percent goal, but we will reach it"
[California SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md). The article
covering SB 1383 is blunt that infrastructure was never the hard part —
households are: "It is an open question of how quickly governments and their
partners can help them connect this threat to what goes in their trash bins,
and convince them to change their habits"
[California SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md).

## What this means for a fast-fashion project

California has now written the textile-waste equivalent of SB 1383 (SB 707),
and SB 1383's five-year track record is a warning, not a template to copy
blindly: the same state, running the same policy tool (mandated collection
infrastructure) for a different waste stream, missed its diversion target
because household behavior didn't follow the infrastructure. Nothing in the
sources says SB 707 will avoid the same outcome — that's a genuine open
question, not a claim either source answers.
