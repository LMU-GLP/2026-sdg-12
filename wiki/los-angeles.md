# Los Angeles / California: organic waste diversion

Source: [Keeping Food Scraps Out of Landfills Is a Bigger Problem Than You
Think](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)

## The law

California's **SB 1383** set the nation's most ambitious organic waste
diversion targets: **"50% below 2014 levels by 2020, and 75% by 2025."**
Regulations were adopted in 2020, and state enforcement began January 2022
([LA / SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)).

## The gap between the law and the outcome

The infrastructure is largely in place — **"75% of California communities"**
report to CalRecycle that they collect residential organic waste. But
collection isn't diversion: ReFED's tracker shows **"over 300,000 MORE tons
of food waste went to California landfills in 2022 than in the year SB 1383
was enacted."** CalRecycle's own director is quoted acknowledging the target
will be missed: *"I don't know when we will reach our 75 percent goal, but
we will reach it."*
([LA / SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md))

## Why: it's a household behavior problem, not an infrastructure problem

The source is explicit that contaminated recycling streams — caused by what
households put in the bin — have made sorting so expensive that processors
have gone out of business. The one intervention described, in Orange County,
was **K–12 curriculum materials** paired with community outreach, aimed
directly at changing what households do, not at building more collection
capacity ([LA / SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)).

## A caveat on geography

The case study behind these numbers is **Orange County**, not the City or
County of Los Angeles specifically. SB 1383 is a statewide law, so the
statewide figures above apply to LA — but there is no LA-specific diversion
number in our sources yet. If the team needs one, CalRecycle's jurisdiction
reports or LA Sanitation are the suggested next source
([LA / SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)).

## Climate stakes

In 38 states, landfills are the largest source of methane emissions, and
methane's warming effect is **80 times greater than CO2** in its first two
decades in the atmosphere. New measurements across 200+ landfills in 18
states suggest landfill emissions may be **1.5 times greater than
previously thought**
([LA / SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)).
