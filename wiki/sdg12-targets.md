# SDG 12 targets that apply to this challenge

Source: [UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md)

Our challenge — *"How might we help a household reduce waste and make
smarter choices about what members buy, use, and throw away?"* — sits
squarely inside three of the UN's targets for Goal 12:

- **Target 12.3** — "By 2030, halve per capita global food waste at the
  retail and consumer levels and reduce food losses along production and
  supply chains, including post-harvest losses." This is the target that
  names the *household* level specifically.
- **Target 12.5** — "By 2030, substantially reduce waste generation through
  prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse." Broader than food — this is
  the target a diversion law like SB 1383 is built to serve.
- **Target 12.8** — "By 2030, ensure that people everywhere have the
  relevant information and awareness for sustainable development and
  lifestyles in harmony with nature." Both the [Los Angeles](los-angeles.md)
  and [South Korea](south-korea.md) pages point to public awareness, not
  infrastructure, as the actual bottleneck — this target names that
  directly.

The official indicators tied to these targets are the food loss and waste
index (12.3.1), national recycling rate (12.5.1), and how much sustainable-
development education is built into national curricula (12.8.1). See the
[source file](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md) for the exact indicator text.
