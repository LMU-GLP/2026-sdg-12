# The scale of the fast fashion problem

Fast fashion — cheap, rapidly-cycled clothing — is a global waste problem
before it's a regional one. The figures below come from several different
organizations, measured different ways and in different years, so they don't
combine into one number. Read them as a range, not an average.

## Emissions and resource use

- Fashion is responsible for somewhere between **2% and 8% of global carbon
  emissions**, depending on the source (World Resources Institute vs. other
  estimates), and could reach **26% of the remaining global carbon budget by
  2050** on current growth (Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017) — three
  different figures, not one
  [Global fast fashion footprint](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md).
- The industry uses an estimated **215 trillion litres of water annually**
  (Quantis, 2018) and is described as the second-largest consumer of water
  globally
  [Global fast fashion footprint](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md).
- An estimated **60% of fashion materials are made from plastic** (synthetic
  fiber), per UNEP, 2019
  [Global fast fashion footprint](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md).

## Waste and recycling

- **85% of all textiles [produced] are dumped annually** (UNECE, 2018), and
  Ellen MacArthur Foundation describes "one garbage truck full of clothes...
  burned or dumped in a landfill every second"
  [Global fast fashion footprint](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md).
- **Less than 1% of the material used to make clothing is recycled back into
  new clothing** (Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017); a separate 2024 estimate
  puts recycled material at only **0.3%** of the industry's total inputs
  (Circularity Gap Report Textiles) — these are two different claims about
  two different things (output recycling vs. input sourcing), not a
  contradiction to average together
  [Global fast fashion footprint](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md).
- **$460 billion in value is lost annually** from clothing discarded while
  still wearable (UN source, 2025)
  [Global fast fashion footprint](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md).
- **500,000 tons of microfibers** enter the ocean every year from washing
  synthetic textiles — equivalent to 50 billion plastic bottles (Ellen
  MacArthur Foundation, 2017); UNEP separately attributes **9% of annual
  ocean microfibre pollution** to textiles
  [Global fast fashion footprint](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md).

## What the UN's own framework does and doesn't say about this

SDG Goal 12 has no clothing- or textile-specific target. Target 12.3 puts a
number on food waste specifically ("halve per capita global food waste... by
2030"), but the target most relevant to fast fashion, Target 12.5
("substantially reduce waste generation through prevention, reduction,
recycling and reuse"), is written in general terms with no textile figure
attached
[UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md). Any fast-fashion-specific
target this team uses in a pitch will be the team's own framing, not a
UN number.
