# Los Angeles vs. South Korea

Sources: [LA / SB 1383](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md),
[Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md),
[UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024](../sources/unep-food-waste-index-2024.md)

Both regions are trying to solve the same problem — a household throwing out
food it should have diverted, composted, or not bought in the first place —
but our sources measure each one differently, and the two are **not**
directly comparable on most of the numbers below. Per CLAUDE.md's rule on
cross-country comparisons, this page says where the comparison breaks down
rather than forcing one.

## What each source actually measures

| | Los Angeles / California | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| What's measured | Diversion **rate target** for organic waste (a law's goal) and actual landfill tonnage vs. target | Recycling **rate achieved** for food waste specifically, plus per-capita generation |
| Figure | Target: 50% below 2014 levels by 2020, 75% by 2025 ([source](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)) | Achieved: 95% of food waste recycled ([source](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)) |
| Status | Missing the target — 300,000+ more tons landfilled in 2022 than in 2016 ([source](../sources/la-sb1383-organic-waste.md)) | System has been running since 1995 (fees) / 2013 (national mandate) ([source](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)) |
| Mechanism | Regulatory mandate + municipal collection infrastructure; enforcement began Jan. 2022 | Volume-based fee (pay by weight) + landfill ban + RFID-metered disposal in cities ([source](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)) |
| Household cost | Not stated in our source | About $6/month ([source](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)) |
| Per-capita food waste generated | Not stated in our LA source — only a statewide diversion percentage exists | 130 kg per person per year ([source](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)) |

## Why these numbers can't be set side by side

- California's figures are about **diversion rate against a target** — a
  law's goal versus outcome. Korea's 95% is a **recycling rate already
  achieved**. A target-vs-actual gap and an achieved rate are not the same
  kind of number.
- Neither of our LA/California sources gives a per-capita food waste
  generation figure comparable to Korea's 130 kg/person/year. The closest
  global figure we have is UNEP's 79 kg/person/year household average
  ([source](../sources/unep-food-waste-index-2024.md)), which is a global
  mean, not a California or US-specific number, and comes from a different
  measurement methodology than Korea's national figure. It would be wrong
  to imply Korea wastes more or less than California per person from what's
  in this wiki.
- The two systems also differ in *what's being asked of the household*:
  California's law asks households to sort waste correctly for collection.
  Korea's system charges households directly by the weight of what they
  throw away. These aren't measuring the same intervention.

## What we can say

Both sources agree on the underlying diagnosis: the technical and
regulatory infrastructure is the easy part, and household behavior is the
hard part. California's source states this directly — collection exists,
diversion doesn't follow. Korea's source frames its own success as evidence
that a visible price signal, staged in over nearly two decades, is what
closed that exact gap. That contrast — mandate-without-price-signal vs.
mandate-with-price-signal — is the one comparison our sources actually
support.
