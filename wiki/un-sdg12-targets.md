# SDG 12 targets, and where fast fashion fits

Goal 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production) has eight numbered targets
and three lettered ones. The two most relevant to a household-waste project
are:

- **Target 12.3:** "By 2030, halve per capita global food waste at the retail
  and consumer levels and reduce food losses along production and supply
  chains, including post-harvest losses."
  [UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md)
- **Target 12.5:** "By 2030, substantially reduce waste generation through
  prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse," measured by national recycling
  rate and tonnage recycled.
  [UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md)

Two others matter for a fast-fashion pitch specifically:

- **Target 12.2** (sustainable management and efficient use of natural
  resources, measured by material footprint) covers the raw-material side of
  clothing production.
  [UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md)
- **Target 12.8** (ensuring people have the awareness for sustainable
  lifestyles) covers the consumer-education angle — relevant if the team's
  solution is about changing what a household buys.
  [UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md)

## The gap

Only Target 12.3 names a specific waste stream (food) with a specific number
(halve it). There is no equivalent "halve textile waste" target. A
fast-fashion project is working under the general umbrella of 12.5, not a
target written with clothing in mind
[UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md). Don't cite a UN textile
target in a pitch — it doesn't exist in this framework.
