# Problem statement

**Region:** Indonesia (Southeast Asia), as the receiving/harmed end of a
problem driven from elsewhere
**Who's driving it:** consumers in leading fast-fashion markets — chiefly
the United States, the world's largest exporter of used clothing and
Shein's single biggest market
**Who's harmed:** Indonesian communities living near textile production and
import-processing sites
**How many:** 185 Central Java residents won a Supreme Court case over one
factory's documented pollution; Indonesia's crackdown on foreign secondhand
imports destroyed 7,000+ bundles ($5.3B) in a single 2023 raid

## What changed, again

Draft 1 was about Indonesia's import-ban affordability tension. Draft 2 moved
to Indonesian households' own overconsumption and disposal habits. The team's
latest guidance moves the causal arrow: the problem isn't Indonesian
households throwing clothes away — it's **leading fast-fashion markets like
the US overconsuming (via platforms like Shein) and exporting the
consequences**, landing as pollution, imports, and waste that Southeast
Asian communities, including in Indonesia, then have to live with. This
draft is built around that direction.

## Part 1: overconsumption in leading markets

- **Shein went from 12% to 50% of US fast-fashion sales between January 2020
  and November 2022** — the only major fast-fashion competitor still
  growing that year, while H&M and Zara each fell 10%
  [Shein/US overconsumption](../sources/shein-us-overconsumption.md).
- Shein adds an estimated **10,000 new items to its site daily**, and its own
  supply-chain emissions hit **11.2 million metric tons of CO2e in 2024**, up
  9.7% from 2023 — a scale a Stand.earth report compares to "the 100th
  biggest emitter [country] in the world"
  [Shein/US overconsumption](../sources/shein-us-overconsumption.md).
- The **United States is the world's largest exporter of used clothing**:
  17.9% of the global market by value, an estimated **700,000 metric tons
  exported annually**
  [US secondhand clothing exports](../sources/us-secondhand-clothing-exports.md).
- The **EU's used-textile exports tripled from 2000 to 2019**, reaching
  almost **1.7 million tonnes**, with **41% going to Asia**; the EEA states
  that unsalvageable material there is **"likely to end up in landfills"**
  [EU used textile exports](../sources/eu-used-textile-exports-to-asia.md).

## Part 2: what lands on Indonesia

- Indonesia banned secondhand clothing imports in **2015** specifically
  because of this influx. In a single raid in West Java in **March 2023**,
  officials destroyed **more than 7,000 bundles** of illegally imported used
  clothing, valued at **$5.3 billion**
  [Indonesia secondhand clothing crackdown](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md).
- Separately, the *production* side that supplies fast fashion's raw
  material is documented as directly harming Indonesian communities: **over
  1,000 factories** on the Citarum River dump mercury, cadmium, and arsenic
  into water locals also bathe and wash clothes in
  [Fast fashion river pollution](../sources/indonesia-fastfashion-river-pollution.md),
  and **185 residents** of Central Java villages won a Supreme Court case
  against a viscose factory after documented air pollution across seven
  subdistricts drove away local wildlife and caused health problems
  [Fast fashion textile factory lawsuit](../sources/indonesia-textile-factory-lawsuit.md).

## The honest gap: what connects Part 1 to Part 2

No source in this wiki proves a direct pipeline — no figure says "X% of the
clothing dumped/burned/imported in Indonesia came from the US" or "from
Shein" specifically:

- The US export source doesn't name Indonesia or any Southeast Asian country
  as a destination
  [US secondhand clothing exports](../sources/us-secondhand-clothing-exports.md).
- The EU export source names Asia broadly (41% of exports) and Pakistan and
  the UAE as its top two named destinations, not Indonesia
  [EU used textile exports](../sources/eu-used-textile-exports-to-asia.md).
- Indonesia's own import-ban source doesn't name which countries the banned
  imports come from
  [Indonesia secondhand clothing crackdown](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md).
- The Citarum River and Central Java pollution cases are about *domestic
  production* of raw material (viscose) for the global fast fashion supply
  chain — real, documented, harmful — but they are not the same thing as
  *imported used clothing from the US* ending up as waste. Global demand
  (including US and Shein demand) for cheap, fast-turnaround clothing is
  what drives that production, but no source quantifies how much of it is
  attributable to US consumers specifically versus any other market.

## The problem, stated at the level our sources can actually defend

Overconsumption in leading fast-fashion markets — the US most of all, where
Shein alone captured half of fast-fashion sales by late 2022 — sits upstream
of two separate, real burdens on Indonesia: (1) a global secondhand-clothing
export glut large enough that Indonesia bans and forcibly destroys it by the
thousands of bundles, and (2) pollution from the raw-material production
(like viscose) that feeds global fast-fashion demand, which has already
caused a Supreme Court-verified case of harm to 185 Central Java residents.
We can defend that leading markets' overconsumption and Indonesia's burden
are both real and connected in kind — we cannot yet defend a specific,
quantified pipeline between the two.

## What we don't have, and shouldn't pretend to

- No source names the US (or any specific country) as an origin for
  Indonesia's imported secondhand clothing or for the raw material behind
  Citarum River or Central Java's pollution.
- No source quantifies what share of Shein's (or any single platform's)
  garments end up as waste, in Indonesia or anywhere else.
- No source gives a total population count of Indonesians affected by
  import-driven or production-driven textile pollution — the only verified
  number is the 185 named plaintiffs in the Central Java case.
