# Problem Statement

Fast fashion bought and discarded by households in South Korea and California/the US ends up as waste that lands on three distinct, named populations in Southeast Asia — Indonesian secondhand-clothing sellers, Cambodian garment-factory workers, and Malaysian consumers — and no country in our research has a mandatory, household-facing system for textiles like the one that already works for food waste.

## The chain

**Source (behavior we want to change):** households in South Korea and California/the US.
**Consequence (who bears the cost):** three separate populations in Southeast Asia, harmed in three different ways. We keep them separate rather than adding their numbers together.

## Why Korea and California

- South Korea discards an estimated **800,000 tonnes of clothing and textiles a year**, only **12%** recycled locally, and exports used clothing mainly to **India, Malaysia, and Pakistan**. [Source](../sources/korea-fast-fashion-boom.md)
- California disposed of **1.2 million tons of textiles in 2021**, 95% of it reusable or recyclable. Its new textile law doesn't require anything of households until **2030**. [Source](../sources/la-sb707-textile-recovery.md)

## Who's affected in Southeast Asia

- **Indonesia:** secondhand-clothing sellers, whose livelihood is threatened by import-ban enforcement — a single 2023 raid destroyed **7,000+ bundles worth $5.3 billion**. [Source](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md)
- **Cambodia:** garment-factory workers exposed to toxic fumes from factory waste (**90,000 tons/year**) burned in brick kilns, made for brands like Nike and Diesel. [Source](../sources/cambodia-fast-fashion-waste-burning.md)
- **Malaysia:** consumers and households on the receiving end of **220,000 tonnes/year** of imported secondhand clothing — one of the world's top importers. [Source](../sources/global-secondhand-clothing-trade-data.md)

## What our sources don't give us

We have volume and dollar figures, not headcounts, for any of these three groups. There's no textile-specific UN target under Goal 12, and no UN program that delivers collection or recycling on the ground — only policy coordination. [Source](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md) · [Source](../sources/un-alliance-sustainable-fashion.md)

## The benchmark

South Korea's own food-waste system recycles **95%** of food waste through a mandatory, pay-by-weight system. [Source](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md) No equivalent exists yet for textiles, in Korea or anywhere else in our research — proof the problem is solvable, not proof it's been solved.
