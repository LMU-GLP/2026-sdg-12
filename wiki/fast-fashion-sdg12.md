# SDG 12 targets applied to fast fashion

Source: [UN SDG 12 targets](../sources/un-sdg12-targets.md)

This wiki's active research track is fast fashion in Southeast Asia. The
same three UN targets that framed this team's earlier food-waste research
apply here too, read against clothing instead of food:

- **Target 12.3** — "By 2030, halve per capita global food waste at the
  retail and consumer levels and reduce food losses along production and
  supply chains, including post-harvest losses." The target's text is
  food-specific, but the household-purchase-decision logic is the same one
  our challenge asks about for clothing: what a household buys, wears, and
  throws away.
- **Target 12.5** — "By 2030, substantially reduce waste generation through
  prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse." This is the target that
  applies most directly to textile waste — it isn't food-specific.
- **Target 12.8** — "By 2030, ensure that people everywhere have the
  relevant information and awareness for sustainable development and
  lifestyles in harmony with nature." This is the target the
  [CASHEW program](../sources/cashew-indonesia-fast-fashion-program.md)
  is built around directly: its own survey found most of its target
  audience didn't know what fast fashion or fashion waste even was.

## Where this shows up elsewhere in the wiki

- [Global scale of fast fashion waste](fast-fashion-global-scale.md) reports
  the production and waste-generation figures Target 12.5 is measured
  against.
- [Southeast Asia: fast fashion](southeast-asia-fast-fashion.md) is a
  12.5-and-12.3-adjacent case: a government waste/industry-protection
  response to secondhand clothing, which is a *reuse* channel, not a waste
  stream — worth noting since 12.5 explicitly names reuse as a goal, and
  Indonesia's policy response cuts against reuse rather than for it.
- [Fast fashion solutions: CASHEW](fast-fashion-solutions.md) is squarely a
  Target 12.8 intervention.

## Caution

No source in this wiki gives an indicator-12.3.1-style food/textile *waste
index* figure specifically for clothing in Southeast Asia. The global
textile waste figures in
[global scale](fast-fashion-global-scale.md) are worldwide totals, not
region-specific — flagged here so the gap isn't papered over later.
