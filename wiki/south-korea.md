# South Korea: pay-by-weight food waste system

Source: [South Korea's Food Waste System is a Model for Developed
Nations](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)

## The headline figure

**"South Korea recycles 95% of its food waste."** Koreans generate an
average of **130 kg of food waste per person per year**, which the source
partly attributes to *banchan*, the many small side dishes served with a
Korean meal ([Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)).

## How the system was built — staged over almost two decades

- **1995:** volume-based waste fee system introduced, making producers pay
  the cost of disposal. Led to a **23% reduction in domestic waste.**
- **2005:** food waste banned from landfills outright.
- **2010:** pilot program in 144 local regions holding residents financially
  accountable for food waste.
- **2013:** national rollout — every resident must dispose of food waste
  properly and **pay for it by weight**, with fees for noncompliance.

Household cost: **about $6 a month**
([Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)).

## Day to day

*"residents in urban areas such as Seoul can take their food waste down to a
bin that opens by RFID chip. The person scans their chip, the lid opens,
they put in their food waste, it is weighed, and the resident is charged
based on the weight."* Outside those areas, residents buy biodegradable bags
for weekly collection
([Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)).

## What happens to the waste

Roughly **80% of collected food waste is liquid**, turned into biogas. The
solid remainder becomes compost and livestock feed
([Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)).

## Why it worked

*"The Korean government prioritized reducing food waste by creating the
infrastructure needed to manage it, taking time to study barriers to
implementation, liaising with restaurant owners, educating the public before
the rollout, and eventually enforcing noncompliance."* Public trust grew
because residents experienced the system working before enforcement arrived
([Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)).

## Caveats

- This article is from **July 2022**; the system description is stable but
  a current recycling rate would need a newer source.
- Figures like "6,000 RFID bins in Seoul" or "96.1 billion won per year in
  economic benefit" circulate elsewhere but are **not** confirmed by this
  source and should not be cited from this wiki
  ([Korea food waste system](../sources/korea-food-waste-system.md)).
