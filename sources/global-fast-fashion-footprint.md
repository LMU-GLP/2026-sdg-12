# Key Environmental Statistics on the Fashion Industry (compilation)

- **Source:** Geneva Environment Network, "Environmental Sustainability in the
  Fashion Industry" (compilation page citing UNEP, Ellen MacArthur Foundation,
  World Resources Institute, UNECE, World Bank, Quantis, and the Circularity
  Gap Report)
- **URL:** https://www.genevaenvironmentnetwork.org/resources/updates/sustainable-fashion/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

Each figure below carries its own original attribution, as given on the page:

- **2-8% of global carbon emissions** come from the fashion industry —
  World Resources Institute.
- Fashion's carbon emissions could account for **26% of the global carbon
  budget by 2050** if current growth continues — Ellen MacArthur Foundation,
  2017.
- The industry uses **215 trillion litres of water annually**, described as
  equivalent to 86 million Olympic-sized swimming pools — Quantis, 2018.
  Fashion is described as the **second-largest consumer of water globally**.
- **85% of all textiles [produced] are dumped annually** — UNECE, 2018.
- *"One garbage truck full of clothes is burned or dumped in a landfill every
  second."* — Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017.
- **$460 billion in annual value loss** from clothing that is discarded while
  still wearable — UN source, 2025.
- **500,000 tons of microfibers** are released into the ocean every year from
  washing synthetic textiles, described as equivalent to 50 billion plastic
  bottles — Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017.
- **9% of annual microfibre [ocean] pollution** originates from textiles —
  UNEP, 2021.
- **Less than 1% of material** used to produce clothing is recycled into new
  clothing — Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017.
- **Only 0.3%** of the fashion industry's material inputs come from recycled
  sources — Circularity Gap Report Textiles, 2024.
- **~20% of industrial wastewater pollution** globally is attributed to
  fashion/textile dyeing and treatment — World Bank, 2020.
- **60% of fashion materials** are made from plastic (synthetic fibers) —
  UNEP, 2019.

## Note on this source

This is a secondary compilation, not a single primary dataset — each bullet
above is one organization's figure, from a different year and methodology.
Don't average or combine them (e.g. the "2-8%" WRI emissions figure and the
"26% by 2050" EMF projection are not the same measure and shouldn't be
quoted as one range).

## Summary

Depending which organization you cite, fashion is responsible for somewhere
between 2% and 8% of current global carbon emissions and could reach 26% of
the remaining carbon budget by 2050, while less than 1% of the material that
goes into clothing is ever recycled back into new clothing. The figures come
from at least seven different named sources on this one page, which is itself
a sign of how little single-source, standardized measurement exists for the
fashion industry's footprint.
