# Saving the Environment with Circular Fashion (Southeast Asia)

- **Source:** The ASEAN Post
- **URL:** https://theaseanpost.com/article/saving-environment-circular-fashion
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **Kloth Cares (Malaysia)** was founded in **2016** under the theme
  "Keeping Fabrics Out of Landfills." The article estimates Malaysians
  produce **almost 2,000 tonnes of textile waste a day.**
- Kloth Cares had collected **over 18,000 kg of fabric by 2017**, and set a
  **2019 collection goal of 188,888 kg.**
- **Style Theory (Singapore)** is a clothing rental service: customers rent a
  dress for an event and return it, rather than buying and discarding.
- **moreloop (Thailand)** connects factories that have leftover ("deadstock")
  fabric with buyers, redistributing material that would otherwise be waste.
- **Fashion Revolution** (the global transparency campaign) has coordinators
  in **nine Southeast Asian countries.**

## Direct quotes

> "based on popular media coverage, it does seem like there is greater

Kimis also said public consciousness of ethical fashion "is still in its
infancy in Malaysia."

## Summary

Southeast Asia already has working models for cutting fast-fashion waste
without waiting for new regulation: Malaysia's Kloth Cares collects discarded
fabric directly (over 18 tonnes in its first year against an estimated 2,000
tonnes of daily textile waste in the country), Singapore's Style Theory
substitutes renting for buying, and Thailand's moreloop redirects factory
deadstock — but the movement's own regional coordinator says public awareness
of the underlying problem is still early-stage.
