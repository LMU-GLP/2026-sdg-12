## Key figures

- Shein's 2024 supply chain emissions: **11,201,419 mtCO2e, a 9.7% jump
  compared to 2023.** Transport/distribution emissions: **8,519,829 mtCO2e,
  up 13.7%** — described as equivalent to nearly 2 million gas-powered
  vehicles driven for a year.
- Per Stand.earth, cited in the Earth.org piece: **"If SHEIN were a country,
  it would be the 100th biggest emitter in the world, almost as much
  pollution as the entire country of Lebanon, having increased Scope 3
  emissions by over 170% in just two years."**
- Production volume: **"up to 10,000 items added to the site each day,"**
  with **"as many as 600,000 items for sale at any given time."**
- **EU Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for textiles took effect
  January 1, 2025:** *"the EU has mandated that textile producers are
  responsible for the entire lifecycle of their products, including
  post-consumer waste,"* requiring companies to *"establish systems for the
  collection, recycling, and proper disposal of textile waste, thereby
  discouraging the practice of destroying unsold goods."*
- Shein's response: a **€200 million Circularity Fund in the EU and UK**,
  meant to **"support startups and businesses focused on textile-to-textile
  recycling innovations, as well as to enhance Shein's own circularity
  efforts."**
- Earlier pledge (June 2022, Copenhagen): **$50 million over five years**
  to tackle textile waste, with a **$15 million initial grant to the Or
  Foundation** (Ghana) to expand its Mabilgu Apprenticeship Program and
  improve conditions at Kantamanto market, Accra — the world's largest
  secondhand clothing market, where Shein-brand clothing has been
  documented arriving as waste.
- Or Foundation director Liz Ricketts praised **"Shein's acknowledgment that
  their clothing may be ending up in Kantamanto, a simple fact no other
  major fashion brand has been willing to state as yet."** A summit
  attendee, anonymously, called the same fund **"public greenwashing"** and
  said **"this is too easy for Shein; it's too soon to call them a
  leader."**
- **Regulatory fines for greenwashing:** Italy's competition authority fined
  Shein **€1 million**, stating its environmental claims were made **"in a
  vague and generic way"** and were **"contradicted by an actual increase
  in Shein's GHG emissions in 2023 and 2024."** France separately fined
  Shein **€40 million** (July 2025) over fake discounts and misleading
  environmental claims.

## Why this matters for ReLoop

This is the evidence base for "why would Shein agree to this," and it's
mixed, not uniformly favorable:

- **The recycling/take-back half of ReLoop has real precedent and real
  incentive alignment.** Shein is already under a legal mandate (EU EPR) to
  build textile collection/recycling systems, is already spending real
  money on circularity PR (€200M fund, $50M pledge), and is already facing
  greenwashing fines that a credible, verifiable diversion program could
  help answer. A recycle-for-reward partnership is the kind of thing Shein
  has already shown willingness to fund.
- **The curated-marketplace half — surfacing other, more sustainable
  brands inside Shein's own checkout — has no precedent in anything found
  here**, and it directly conflicts with Shein's core revenue model in a way
  the recycling half doesn't. Nothing in this source suggests Shein has ever
  agreed to promote competitor products to its own customers.
- All of the regulatory pressure documented here is **EU/UK-specific**
  (EU EPR law, Italian and French fines). None of it is Indonesian or
  Southeast Asian regulation — see
  [Shein's Indonesia market status](shein-indonesia-market-status.md) for
  why that geography mismatch matters.

## Summary

Shein is under real, escalating EU regulatory and reputational pressure over
textile waste — a 2025 EU law now legally requires producer-funded
collection and recycling systems, and Shein has already been fined by two
countries for exaggerating its environmental claims. Its responses so far
(a €200M circularity fund, a $50M textile-waste pledge including money to
Ghana's Kantamanto market) show it already treats "we help with recycling"
as a viable PR and compliance move — but nothing in this record suggests
Shein would ever agree to promote rival, more-sustainable brands to its own
shoppers, since that has no precedent and cuts against its own sales.
