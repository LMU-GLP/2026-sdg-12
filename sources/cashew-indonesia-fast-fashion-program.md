# CASHEW (Cut Fast Fashion and Waste) — CIMSA-Indonesia

- **Source:** International Federation of Medical Students' Associations
  (IFMSA), program page for CASHEW, run by CIMSA-Indonesia (the Indonesian
  member association)
- **URL:** https://ifmsa.org/cashew-cut-fast-fashion-and-waste-cimsa-indonesia/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **Launched June 18, 2023.**
- Target audience: ages **17–25**, general population and youth.
- Activities: **"pre-activity training, webinars, small working groups,
  talkshow, air campaigns, and challenge."**
- Pre-activity training: **89.66 average post-test score.**
- Webinar ("Revolutionizing The Fashion Industry to Reduce Climate Change"):
  **359 participants.**
- Small working group discussions: **364 participants.**
- Instagram live session: **357 participants.**
- Instagram "Outfit of the Day" challenge: **73 participants.**
- Baseline survey finding: **"more than 50% didn't know about fast fashion
  and fashion waste"** despite recognizing environmental danger, and
  **"70% of respondents do not know how to overcome it."**
- States **Indonesia ranks second globally in textile waste contribution**
  — this claim is not independently corroborated by the global dataset
  source in this wiki ([global fast fashion
  stats](../sources/global-fast-fashion-waste-stats.md)), which has no
  country ranking; flagged rather than merged.

## Note on this source

This is a youth-led public health/education initiative (run through a
medical students' association), not a government program or a business —
its "solution" is awareness-building (education, campaigns) rather than
infrastructure or a market mechanism. That maps directly onto SDG Target
12.8 (public information and awareness), separately from Target 12.3/12.5
which the waste-diversion-style sources in this wiki address.

## Summary

CASHEW is a 2023 youth education campaign by CIMSA-Indonesia that found,
via its own baseline survey, that more than half of its target audience
didn't know what fast fashion or fashion waste was, and 70% didn't know how
to address it — then ran webinars, workshops, and social media campaigns
reaching several hundred participants each to close that gap. It's a small-
scale, awareness-first intervention rather than a market or policy fix, and
it's the only source in this wiki that measures the *awareness* gap
directly rather than a waste-tonnage or diversion-rate gap.
