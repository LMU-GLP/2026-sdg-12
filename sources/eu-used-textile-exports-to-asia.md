# EU Exports of Used Textiles

- **Source:** European Environment Agency (EEA)
- **URL:** https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/eu-exports-of-used-textiles
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- EU used-textile exports **tripled over two decades**: from **just over
  550,000 tonnes in 2000** to **almost 1.7 million tonnes in 2019.**
- That's **3.8 kg per EU resident in 2019** — described as **25% of the
  approximately 15 kg of textiles** the average EU resident consumes each
  year.
- **Regional destination (2019): Africa 46%, Asia 41%,** non-EU Europe
  ~10% (constant over time).
- Top individual receiving countries named in the report: **Pakistan
  (1st), UAE (2nd).** The report's own top-10 list, as extracted, does not
  name Indonesia, Malaysia, or any other Southeast Asian country
  specifically.
- Average export price fell to about **€0.57/kg by 2019** (constant
  prices) — a sign of falling quality/resale value in what's being
  exported.

## What happens to it, per the EEA

- In Africa: mostly reused directly, due to demand for affordable European
  clothing; unfit items "mostly [end] up in open landfills and informal
  waste streams."
- In Asia: imported into "dedicated economic zones where they are sorted
  and processed," with most "downcycled into industrial rags or filling, or
  re-exported for recycling in other Asian countries or for reuse in
  Africa." Unsalvageable textiles are "likely to end up in landfills."

## Note on scope

This is EU-wide data, not US data, and "Asia" here is not broken into
countries beyond the Pakistan/UAE top-2 in the summary extracted. It
supports the general claim that wealthy exporting regions' overconsumption
creates a textile-waste stream flowing toward Asia, much of it ending in
landfill — but it does not name Indonesia or confirm Indonesia is a direct
recipient of EU exports specifically.

## Summary

The EU's used-textile exports tripled between 2000 and 2019 to nearly 1.7
million tonnes a year, with 41% going to Asia, where the EEA says
unsalvageable material is "likely to end up in landfills" — solid evidence
that wealthy-region overconsumption is driving a textile-waste stream toward
Asia broadly, though this source doesn't confirm Indonesia specifically as a
landing point.
