# Keeping Food Scraps Out of Landfills Is a Bigger Problem Than You Think

- **Source:** Carl Smith, *Governing*, April 25, 2024
- **URL:** https://www.governing.com/resilience/keeping-food-scraps-out-of-landfills-is-a-bigger-problem-than-you-think
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- California's **SB 1383** set the nation's most ambitious organic waste diversion targets: **50% below 2014 levels by 2020, and 75% by 2025.** Regulations adopted 2020; state enforcement began January 2022.
- **75% of California communities** report to CalRecycle that they collect residential organic waste. Diversion rates are a different story.
- ReFED's tracker shows **over 300,000 MORE tons of food waste went to California landfills in 2022 than in the year SB 1383 was enacted.**
- **In 38 states, landfills are the largest source of methane emissions.**
- Methane's warming effect is **80 times greater than CO2** during its first two decades in the atmosphere.
- New airborne-spectrometer measurements across 200+ landfills in 18 states suggest landfill emissions may be **1.5 times greater than previously thought.**

## The behavior problem

The article is blunt that infrastructure isn't the hard part — households are.
Contaminated recycling streams have already made sorting so expensive that many
processors went out of business. Orange County's response was to work with the
county education department on **K–12 curriculum materials** for classroom
lessons and family activities, backed by community outreach.

## ⚠️ Geographic note

The case study here is **Orange County**, not Los Angeles County. SB 1383 is a
statewide law that applies to LA identically, and the statewide figures above
hold. But if this team wants LA-specific diversion numbers, that's a good target
for Friday's source sprint — try CalRecycle's jurisdiction reports or LA
Sanitation.

## Summary

California set the country's most ambitious food waste diversion targets and is
going to miss them badly — more food waste went to landfills in 2022 than when
the law passed. The infrastructure largely exists and the collection programs
are running; what hasn't changed is what households actually put in the bin.
