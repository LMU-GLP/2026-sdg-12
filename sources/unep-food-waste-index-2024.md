# Food Waste Index Report 2024 (UNEP)

- **Source:** UN Environment Programme (UNEP) and WRAP, *Food Waste Index
  Report 2024: Think Eat Save*, March 2024. Summarized via BioCycle,
  "UN's 2024 Food Waste Index Attributes 60% Of Food Waste To Households,"
  and UNEP press release "World Squanders Over 1 Billion Meals a Day."
- **URL:** https://www.unep.org/resources/publication/food-waste-index-report-2024
  (report landing page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch; figures below
  drawn from https://www.biocycle.net/2024-food-waste-index/, a secondary
  summary of the same report)
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- In 2022, **"the world wasted 1.05 billion metric tons (1.16 billion tons)
  of food — amounting to one-fifth (19%) of food available to consumers."**
- Households were responsible for **"631 million metric tons, equivalent to
  60%"** of that total.
- Food service and retail combined accounted for **"421 million metric tons,
  or 40% (290 and 131 million metric tons, respectively)."**
- **"On average, each person wastes 79kg of food annually"** — this is the
  household per-capita figure.
- **"Food loss and waste generates 8% to 10% of global greenhouse gas (GHG)
  emissions — almost five times the total emissions from the aviation
  sector."**
- U.S. regional example cited in the report: the Pacific Coast Food Waste
  Commitment achieved **"190,000 tons of food valued at $311 million"**
  reduction from 2019–2022.
- The 2024 report reflects **"a near doubling of data coverage since the
  2021 Food Waste Index Report was published,"** though the exact country
  count was not stated in the secondary source used here.

## Note on regional comparability

This dataset gives a **global average of 79 kg of household food waste per
person per year**. South Korea's source in this wiki reports **130 kg per
person per year** — a national figure using Korea's own accounting, not the
UNEP methodology. These two numbers are not necessarily measuring waste the
same way (see `wiki/la-vs-south-korea.md` for the caveat) and should not be
subtracted or averaged against each other without checking the underlying
methodology.

## Summary

UNEP's 2024 Food Waste Index Report finds that the world wasted 1.05 billion
metric tons of food in 2022 — 19% of all food available to consumers — and
that households, not retailers or restaurants, generated 60% of it at 79 kg
per person per year. Food loss and waste together produce 8–10% of global
greenhouse gas emissions, nearly five times aviation's share.
