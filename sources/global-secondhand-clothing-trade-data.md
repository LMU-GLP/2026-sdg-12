# Global Trade of Secondhand Clothing (UN Comtrade figures, via Wikipedia)

- **Source:** Wikipedia, "Global trade of secondhand clothing," citing UN Comtrade data
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_trade_of_secondhand_clothing
- **Read on:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures

- Secondhand clothing **"accounts for approximately 0.5% of the total value of clothing traded worldwide, while by weight it accounts for 5%"** — a large physical volume relative to its dollar value.
- Largest net exporters (2019): **the United States, followed by Germany, the United Kingdom and China.**
- Largest importers by weight, **2015–2019, UN Comtrade data:**
  - **Pakistan: 640,000 tonnes/year**
  - **India: 330,000 tonnes/year**
  - **Malaysia: 220,000 tonnes/year** — the only Southeast Asian country in this top tier
  - **Angola: 190,000 tonnes/year**
- Of clothing donated in exporting countries, **"usually no more than 10%, is sold domestically"** — the rest is exported or routed to textile recycling companies for sorting, recycling, landfill disposal, or resale abroad.
- The article describes "waste colonialism" as a live controversy: some countries resent being treated as **a "dumping ground"** for discarded clothing by former colonial powers, which the article says adds **"a political dimension"** to the trade.

## Note on scope

This is the most specific country-level import figure in this wiki tying a Southeast Asian country directly to the secondhand-clothing trade: Malaysia imports an estimated 220,000 tonnes/year, on par with Angola and behind only Pakistan and India globally. The article does not give separate tonnage figures for Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam, or Cambodia — that is a gap, not a zero.

## Summary

Secondhand clothing trades globally at a volume out of proportion to its value — 5% of clothing's traded weight but only 0.5% of its traded value — and Malaysia is one of the world's top four importers by tonnage at roughly 220,000 tonnes a year, per UN Comtrade data, while only about 10% of the original donations are ever resold in the exporting country itself, meaning the rest is shipped abroad or discarded.
