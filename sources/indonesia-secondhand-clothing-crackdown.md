# Indonesia's War on Secondhand Clothes Riles Local Sellers

- **Source:** Al Jazeera
- **URL:** https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2023/4/11/indonesias-war-on-secondhand-clothes-riles-local-sellers
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- Indonesia has **banned secondhand clothing imports since 2015**, to
  protect its domestic textile industry.
- In late March 2023, officials destroyed **more than 7,000 bundles** of
  illegally imported secondhand clothing in West Java, valued at
  **$5.3 billion.**
- More than **26 million people** are officially recorded as living below
  Indonesia's poverty line — the population the secondhand-clothing trade
  serves and that the crackdown most directly affects.
- Seller Siti reported earning roughly **$536** from selling used garments
  every few weeks.

## Direct quotes

> "This is a follow-up to a directive from the President. We have done this
> several times and this is the climax." — Trade Minister Zulkifli Hasan

> "I hope there will still be stock available. If there are no clothes, that
> means no money." — Siti, secondhand clothing seller

> "It gives people who don't have the means to buy branded clothes at full
> price the chance to still own a piece." — Kosman Samosir, business law
> lecturer

## Note on what this does and doesn't show

This article is about enforcement of an *import ban*, not about textile waste
volume directly — it doesn't state what share of destroyed imports would
otherwise have become landfill waste versus resale stock. For the "unsold
imports become waste" figure, see the separate reporting on Indonesia's
secondhand clothing supply chain (not yet a saved source in this repo — flag
as a gap if the team wants that number).

## Summary

Indonesia has banned secondhand clothing imports since 2015 specifically to
protect its own textile industry, and enforcement is visible and dramatic — a
single 2023 raid destroyed over 7,000 bundles worth a reported $5.3 billion —
but the ban puts the government directly at odds with sellers like Siti, for
whom used clothing is livelihood, not waste.
