# Indonesia's War on Secondhand Clothes Riles Local Sellers

- **Source:** Al Jazeera, April 11, 2023 (figures cross-checked against
  ANTARA News, "Ministry destroys imported secondhand clothes worth Rp10
  bln," and Tempo, "Indonesia Destroys Rp174.8bn Illegally Imported Goods in
  2023," for the rupiah/dollar conversion)
- **URL:** https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2023/4/11/indonesias-war-on-secondhand-clothes-riles-local-sellers
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **Indonesia banned secondhand clothing imports in 2015**, citing public
  health and domestic textile industry protection.
- In **late March 2023**, trade minister Zulkifli Hasan oversaw the
  destruction of illegally imported used clothes across **more than 7,000
  bundles** in West Java. Cross-referenced reporting puts the market value
  destroyed at **roughly 80 billion rupiah (about $5.3 million)** — note
  that the Al Jazeera article itself states this figure in a way that reads
  as "$5.3bn," which does not match the rupiah figure or other outlets'
  reporting; treat the rupiah figure and the ~$5.3 million conversion as the
  reliable number.
- **Over 26 million Indonesians live officially below the poverty line**,
  which the article cites as part of the demand for affordable secondhand
  clothing sourced from Korea, Japan, and Singapore.
- A Medan vendor, Siti, typically sells **about $536 worth of garments every
  few weeks** from secondhand stock.

## Direct quotes

> "used clothing presents a risk as it could carry bacteria or fungi"
> — Rio Priambodo, Indonesian Consumer Foundation, also citing environmental
> concern that low-quality secondhand items become landfill waste.

> "If there are no clothes, that means no money." — Siti, secondhand clothing
> vendor in Medan.

> "importers are scared their wares will be seized and incinerated."
> — Hotnida Sianturi, on sourcing difficulty after the crackdown.

## Note on figure discrepancy

The $5.3 million/$5.3 billion mismatch across outlets is noted above rather
than resolved by picking one — see CLAUDE.md rule on keeping disagreeing
sources rather than averaging them. The rupiah figure (80 billion) and the
million-dollar conversion are corroborated by two independent Indonesian
outlets, which is why they're treated as more reliable here.

## Summary

Indonesia has banned secondhand clothing imports since 2015 and destroyed
over 7,000 bundles of illegally imported used clothes worth roughly $5.3
million in a March 2023 enforcement action, framed as protecting public
health and the domestic textile industry. The crackdown collides directly
with household demand: secondhand clothing is the affordable option for a
population where more than 26 million people live below the poverty line,
and local vendors say the enforcement is cutting off their livelihood, not
just an import stream.
