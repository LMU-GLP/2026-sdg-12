# Shein's US Market Share Growth, and Its Production Scale

- **Source:** Bloomberg Second Measure (market share data) and Earth.Org,
  citing a Stand.earth emissions report (production/emissions data)
- **URL:** https://secondmeasure.com/datapoints/fast-fashion-market-share-us-consumer-spending-data-shein-hm-zara/
  and https://earth.org/fast-fashion-giant-sheins-emissions-balloon-in-2024/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures — US market share (Bloomberg Second Measure)

- Shein held **12% of US fast fashion sales in January 2020**; by
  **November 2022, that had grown to 50%** — the largest share of any
  fast-fashion competitor.
- For comparison, in November 2022: **H&M 16%, Zara 13%, Fashion Nova 11%,
  Forever 21 6%, ASOS 4%.**
- Shein's US sales were the only ones among these companies still growing
  year-over-year in November 2022 (**+9%**), while H&M and Zara each fell
  **-10%** and Fashion Nova fell **-25%.**
- Shein's US sales **roughly doubled between March and April 2020.**
- Shein was valued at **$100 billion** after an April 2022 funding round.

## Key figures — production and emissions scale (Earth.Org / Stand.earth)

- Shein adds an estimated **10,000 new items to its site daily**, with up to
  **600,000 items** available for sale at any given time, at an average
  price of **$10 per item.**
- Design-to-garment turnaround is **as little as 10 days.**
- Shein's supply chain emissions were **11.2 million metric tons of CO2e in
  2024**, a **9.7% increase from 2023**; total scope 3 emissions rose
  **over 170% in a two-year period.**
- Stand.earth's report: **"If SHEIN were a country, it would be the 100th
  biggest emitter in the world, almost as much pollution as the entire
  country of Lebanon."**

## Note on scope

Neither source states where Shein's discarded garments physically end up —
this is evidence of *scale of production and US consumption*, not a documented
supply chain from a specific US Shein order to a specific landfill or country.
Don't cite this as proof that Shein-specific waste ends up in Indonesia or
Southeast Asia; no source in this wiki makes that direct link.

## Summary

Shein went from 12% to 50% of US fast-fashion sales between January 2020 and
November 2022 — the only major competitor still growing — while adding an
estimated 10,000 new items a day and increasing its own supply-chain
emissions by nearly 10% in 2024 alone; a Stand.earth report compares its
total emissions to those of a mid-sized country. This is direct evidence of
US overconsumption of ultra-fast fashion, not evidence of where that
consumption's waste physically travels afterward.
