# Algramo: Smart Packaging Is Driving the Refill Revolution

- **URL:** https://www.weforum.org/stories/2021/04/algramo-reusable-smart-packaging/
- **Published:** April 21, 2021 (World Economic Forum)
- **Logged:** 2026-07-29
- **Source category:** Product/program already trying to solve this problem
- **Regions:** South America (Chile — origin/HQ) and Southeast Asia (Indonesia — active market)

## Key facts & figures

- Algramo was founded in 2013 in Chile by José Manuel Moller to fight the "poverty tax" — the extra per-unit cost low-income households pay for buying goods in small packages.
- The system uses RFID-tagged reusable containers and IoT-connected dispensers so households pay for product "by the gram" instead of paying for new packaging each time.
- In Chile, refills are delivered to customers' doorsteps by electric tricycle; households order and pay through the Algramo app.
- Scale in Chile: reaches more than 1,700 family-owned neighborhood stores, with the potential to reach over 275,000 end consumers.
- Plastic-reduction evidence from a Unilever pilot: "The Unilever pilot saw some customers refill their detergent bottle 15 times, with each refill eliminating the need to recycle or send to landfill a 135g plastic bottle — removing the need for just over 2kg of plastic."
- Expansion: "Algramo already has pilot programs in the US and Indonesia, and is looking to enter new markets including Mexico and the UK." The company is now operating refill programs in Santiago, New York City, and Jakarta.
- Indonesia specifics (via partner QYOS, using Algramo's dispenser technology): Unilever refill stations dispensing Rinso and Sunlight home-care products let shoppers save up to 20% versus retail shelf prices.

## Two-sentence summary

Algramo is a Chilean startup, founded in 2013, that replaces single-use plastic packaging with an app- and RFID-based refill system for household staples like detergent, rice, and pet food — delivered door-to-door by electric tricycle in Chile and dispensed through partner-run in-store refill stations in Indonesia. It is a working example of a product-level intervention for SDG 12: it cuts a household's plastic packaging waste on repeat, everyday purchases while also lowering costs for lower-income shoppers, and it already operates in both of our candidate regions, South America and Southeast Asia.
