# Fast Fashion: Indonesian Consumer Disposal Behavior

- **Source:** YouGov
- **URL:** https://yougov.com/articles/19824-fast-fashion-4
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **66%** of Indonesian adults threw away clothes within the past year.
- **25%** discarded more than 10 clothing items in that year.
- **29%** threw away a garment after wearing it just once.
- **15%** disposed of at least 3 single-wear items in a year.
- Disposal methods: **66%** passed clothes to friends/family, **50%**
  donated to charity — but **5% of millennials reported burning unwanted
  clothing** (vs. 1% of baby boomers).
- Millennials buy and discard faster than older generations: **41%** of
  millennials bought at least half their wardrobe within 12 months (vs. 17%
  of boomers), and **17%** of millennials keep clothes under one year before
  disposal (vs. 10% of boomers). **21%** of millennials say they discard
  clothes out of boredom (vs. 16% of boomers).
- Survey basis: YouGov Omnibus, **7,439 online responses**, collected
  **October 20–30, 2017**, representing Indonesia's adult online population.

## ⚠️ Date note

This survey is from **December 2017** — over eight years old as of this
wiki. Fast fashion consumption has almost certainly grown since then (see
[South Korea's fast fashion boom](korea-fast-fashion-boom.md) for a 2024-25
example of the trend elsewhere); if the team needs a current Indonesian
figure, this one should be treated as a floor, not a current reading.

## Summary

In a 2017 YouGov survey, two-thirds of Indonesian adults said they had
thrown away clothing in the past year, 29% had discarded a garment after
wearing it only once, and a small but real share of millennials (5%) said
they burned unwanted clothes outright — direct evidence that Indonesian
household disposal behavior, not just import policy, is a driver of the
country's textile waste.
