# UN Alliance for Sustainable Fashion

- **Source:** fashionabc.org (wiki page compiling UNEP/UN Alliance for Sustainable Fashion materials)
- **URL:** https://www.fashionabc.org/wiki/un-alliance-for-sustainable-fashion/
- **Read on:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures

- The Alliance **launched in March 2019** at the UN Environment Assembly in Nairobi, Kenya.
- Its **eight founding member organizations**: Connect4Climate (World Bank Group), the International Labour Organisation, the ITC Ethical Fashion Initiative, the UN Development Programme, the UN Economic Commission for Europe, UN Environment, UN Global Compact, and the United Nations Office for Partnerships.
- Its mandate is to **"coordinate the U.N. system's response to the challenges that textiles, fashion and accessories sectors face in achieving the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development."**
- Fashion is described as **"the second-biggest consumer of water,"** generating **"around 20 percent of the world's wastewater,"** and releasing **"half a million tons of synthetic microfibers into the ocean annually."**
- **"2,700 liters"** of water are used to produce one shirt.
- The industry is responsible for **"8-10 percent of global carbon emissions"** and uses **"24 percent of insecticides and 11 percent of pesticides"** (share of global use, as stated on the page).
- Fashion **"employs approximately 60 million people worldwide."**

## What the Alliance actually does

Rather than running its own programs, the Alliance's stated method is to have member agencies identify **"gaps"** in existing UN action on fashion sustainability, then use that analysis to shape future UN initiatives, improve existing schemes, and lobby national governments to change policy — a coordination and analysis body, not a delivery program with its own projects on the ground.

## Note on this source

This page compiles and paraphrases UNEP/UN Alliance materials rather than being the primary UNEP press release itself (unep.org blocked automated access when checked). The figures above — 8-10% of global carbon emissions, half a million tons of microfibers, 2,700 liters per shirt — overlap with, but are not identical to, the figures already collected in [global fast-fashion footprint](global-fast-fashion-footprint.md) (e.g. that source's "2-8%" WRI emissions figure vs. this page's "8-10%," and its "500,000 tons" EMF microfiber figure, which matches this page's "half a million tons"). Treat overlapping figures as separate citations from separate compilations, not as one confirmed number.

## Summary

The UN Alliance for Sustainable Fashion, launched in March 2019 by eight UN and partner agencies, doesn't run its own textile-waste programs — its job is to find gaps in what the UN system is already doing on fashion sustainability and push that analysis into new initiatives and national policy, which means it's a coordination mechanism rather than a source of new funding, infrastructure, or collection systems on the ground in places like Southeast Asia.
