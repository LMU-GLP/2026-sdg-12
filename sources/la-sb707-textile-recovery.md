# California's SB 707: The Responsible Textile Recovery Act of 2024

- **Source:** Fashion Dive (staff), reporting on California SB 707
- **URL:** https://www.fashiondive.com/news/california-responsible-textile-recovery-act-passed/728826/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **In 2021, approximately 1.2 million tons of textiles were disposed of in
  California**, according to the office of state Senator Josh Newman, despite
  **95% [of that material] being reusable or recyclable.**
- SB 707 was **signed into law the week of October 3, 2024**, and is
  described as **"the nation's first extended producer responsibility program
  for textiles."**
- **PRO (Producer Responsibility Organization) approval deadline: March 1,
  2026.**
- **Program implementation begins: July 1, 2028.**
- **Collection/repair/recycling plan due: July 1, 2030.**
- Covered products: apparel, footwear, swimwear, undergarments, handbags, and
  other textile articles "unsuitable for reuse in current condition."

## Direct quotes

> "the nation's first extended producer responsibility program for textiles"

Senator Newman's office framed the law as addressing "the 'throwaway
culture'" driven by fast fashion and its carbon emissions.

## What the law requires

Apparel and textile producers operating in California must form or join a
PRO, approved by CalRecycle, that develops a statewide plan covering
collection, transportation, sorting, repair, and recycling of textiles. It is
structurally the same policy tool — extended producer responsibility — that
[South Korea's food waste system](korea-food-waste-system.md) used for organic
waste, applied here to clothing instead.

## ⚠️ Timeline note

This is a very slow rollout: the law passed in October 2024, but the first
producer plan isn't due until **July 1, 2030** — six years after signing.
Nothing about actual textile collection changes for California households
before then under this law.

## Summary

California passed the country's first extended-producer-responsibility law
for textiles in 2024 after finding that 1.2 million tons of textiles were
thrown away in the state in a single year (2021), 95% of which didn't need to
be — but the law's own implementation timeline stretches to 2030, meaning it
will not change what a Los Angeles household does with old clothes any time
soon.
