# Cambodian Workers Pay the Price of Fast Fashion's Waste Problem

- **Source:** Greenpeace Southeast Asia
- **URL:** https://www.greenpeace.org/southeastasia/story/45543/cambodian-workers-pay-the-price-of-fast-fashions-waste-problem/
- **Read on:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures

- Cambodia's garment industry generates **"no less than 90,000 tons of garment waste per year."**
- Garment waste made for **Nike, Ralph Lauren, Diesel, and Clarks** is being burned in Cambodian brick kilns, per the underlying Unearthed investigation cited by Greenpeace.
- Kilns operate around the clock, **"reaching up to 650C,"** burning a mix of garment waste and wood as fuel.
- **"For fashion consumed in the EU, 84.7% of the environmental impacts take place outside the EU, mostly in Asia."**

## Direct quotes

> "the burning of this plastic exposes bonded workers to toxic fumes and microplastic fibres" — most garments are polyester, so burning them is effectively burning plastic.

> Workers in these kiln arrangements are described as "bonded" and engaged in "a form of modern-day slavery."

> Brands' "claims of zero-waste and worker standards in supply chains... are no more than greenwashing" without supply-chain transparency, per the investigation's conclusion.

## Note on what this does and doesn't show

This is about production-side waste — garment factory offcuts and rejects burned domestically in Cambodia — not imported secondhand clothing from consumers abroad. It's a different waste stream than the used-clothing exports covered in [global secondhand clothing trade data](global-secondhand-clothing-trade-data.md) and [EU used textile exports](eu-used-textile-exports.md), but it lands on the same population and makes the same point: the environmental and human cost of garments made for developed-country brands is disproportionately paid outside the country where the clothing is bought and worn.

## Summary

Cambodia's garment industry generates at least 90,000 tons of waste a year, and an investigation found offcuts and rejects made for Nike, Ralph Lauren, Diesel, and Clarks being burned in brick kilns by bonded workers exposed to toxic fumes and microplastics — evidence for Greenpeace's broader point that 84.7% of the environmental impact of fashion consumed in the EU falls outside the EU, mostly in Asia.
