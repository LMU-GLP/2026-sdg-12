# Indonesia's Fast Fashion Pollution Problems Fueled by Consumer Demand

- **Source:** CGTN Europe
- **URL:** https://newseu.cgtn.com/news/2021-06-26/Indonesia-s-fast-fashion-pollution-problems-fueled-by-consumer-demand-11ohJceggmc/index.html
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- Indonesia produces **2.4% of global textiles.**
- **Over 1,000 factories** operate along the banks of the **Citarum River**,
  discharging toxic chemicals — including **mercury, cadmium, and arsenic**
  — into water that locals also use to bathe and wash clothes in.
- Manufacturing **viscose fibers** (made from wood pulp, used by the fast
  fashion industry) generates **carbon disulfide gas**, which can leak into
  the air; the process also produces sulfuric acid and zinc, which companies
  "often pump and dump into the nearest waterway."
- Global figures cited in the piece (not Indonesia-specific): **92 million
  tonnes** of textile waste created annually, projected to reach **134
  million tonnes by 2030**; the fashion industry is described as responsible
  for around **10% of global greenhouse gas emissions** — more than aviation
  and maritime shipping combined, per UK Textile Recycling Association
  director Alan Wheeler.

## Note on scope

This article ties fast fashion's *raw-material production* (viscose
manufacturing for garment supply chains) to river pollution in West Java —
it is a supply-side pollution story, not a story about what happens to
clothes after a household throws them away. Don't cite the 92-million-tonne
or 10%-of-emissions figures as Indonesia-specific; they are global figures
repeated in this article, not measured in Indonesia.

## Summary

More than 1,000 factories along Indonesia's Citarum River — supplying
material, including viscose, to the fast fashion industry — dump toxic
chemicals including mercury, cadmium, and arsenic into water that local
residents also use to bathe and wash clothes, according to Nexus3
Foundation's Yuyun Ismawati, who says the industry is causing both
environmental damage and health problems.
