# International Organizations' Response

Where the UN and its partner agencies sit in this problem — a level above the on-the-ground programs in [existing solutions](existing-solutions.md), and the gap this leaves for a household-facing project.

## The UN Alliance for Sustainable Fashion

- **Launched in March 2019** at the UN Environment Assembly in Nairobi, Kenya, by **eight founding member organizations**: Connect4Climate (World Bank Group), the International Labour Organisation, the ITC Ethical Fashion Initiative, the UN Development Programme, the UN Economic Commission for Europe, UN Environment, UN Global Compact, and the United Nations Office for Partnerships.
- Its mandate: **"coordinate the U.N. system's response to the challenges that textiles, fashion and accessories sectors face in achieving the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development."**
- Cited figures on fashion's footprint: **"the second-biggest consumer of water,"** **"around 20 percent of the world's wastewater,"** **"half a million tons of synthetic microfibers into the ocean annually,"** **"2,700 liters"** of water per shirt, **"8-10 percent of global carbon emissions,"** **"24 percent of insecticides and 11 percent of pesticides"** (share of global use), and **"approximately 60 million people"** employed worldwide.
[Source](../sources/un-alliance-sustainable-fashion.md)

## What the Alliance actually does — and doesn't do

By its own stated method, the Alliance doesn't run textile-collection or recycling programs. Its job is to have member agencies identify **"gaps"** in existing UN action on fashion sustainability, then feed that analysis into new UN initiatives, improve existing schemes, and lobby national governments to change policy. It is a **coordination and analysis body**, not a delivery mechanism with its own projects on the ground. [Source](../sources/un-alliance-sustainable-fashion.md)

That distinction matters directly for this wiki: none of the figures in [Southeast Asia: where developed-country fashion waste lands](southeast-asia-impacts.md) — Malaysia's 220,000 tonnes/year of imports, Cambodia's 90,000 tons/year of burned factory waste, Indonesia's crackdowns — trace back to any action by the Alliance. It exists at the policy-coordination level; the waste itself is being handled (or not) by national governments, private exporters, and small NGOs, as documented in [existing solutions](existing-solutions.md).

## Reading the figures against what's already in this wiki

The Alliance's own stats overlap with, but are not identical to, the ones in [global fast-fashion footprint](global-fast-fashion-footprint.md):

- **8-10% of global carbon emissions** (Alliance) vs. **2-8%** (World Resources Institute, in the footprint page) — different ranges, different attributions. Don't average them into one number.
- **Half a million tons of microfibers** (Alliance) matches the **500,000 tons** figure already attributed to the Ellen MacArthur Foundation — likely the same underlying estimate cited by both, but each source should still be cited separately.

## Why this matters for a fast-fashion project under Goal 12

[UN SDG 12 targets](un-sdg12-targets.md) has no textile-specific target or number. The Alliance is the closest thing to an operational answer to that gap — a body that exists specifically because Goal 12 doesn't name fashion — but its own scope is policy coordination, not the mandatory, metered, household-facing system that worked for [Korea's food waste](household-behavior-lessons.md). That gap between "the UN has a coordinating body for this" and "no country in this wiki has a working household-facing textile-waste system" is the space a project can occupy.
