# Wiki index — SDG 12: fast fashion's impact on Southeast Asia

Challenge: *How might we help a household reduce waste and make smarter choices
about what members buy, use, and throw away?* Focus: **the problems and
effects in Southeast Asia caused by fast fashion and overconsumption of
clothes in developed countries.**

Start with [the scale of the problem](global-fast-fashion-footprint.md), then
[what developed countries consume and export](developed-country-drivers.md),
then the core page — [what lands in Southeast Asia](southeast-asia-impacts.md)
— then [existing solutions](existing-solutions.md),
[the international response](international-response.md), and the
[behavior lessons](household-behavior-lessons.md) they point to.

## Problem scope

| Page | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| [Global fast-fashion footprint](global-fast-fashion-footprint.md) | The industry's worldwide emissions, water use, and waste figures — several different organizations' numbers, which don't average into one figure. |
| [UN SDG 12 targets](un-sdg12-targets.md) | Goal 12 has a specific number for food waste but none for textiles; fast fashion sits under the general "waste generation" target. |

## Developed countries: the demand and export side

| Page | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| [Developed-country consumption and export patterns](developed-country-drivers.md) | South Korea's $58B fashion spending and 800,000 t/year discarded, California's 1.2 million tons disposed in 2021, and the EU's 1.44 million tonnes exported in 2025 — separate figures that share one pattern: high consumption, low domestic recycling, growing exports. |

## Southeast Asia: where it lands

| Page | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| [Southeast Asia: where developed-country fashion waste lands](southeast-asia-impacts.md) | Two distinct waste streams — Malaysia's 220,000 t/year of imported secondhand clothing and Indonesia's import-ban crackdowns on one side; Cambodia's 90,000 t/year of factory offcuts from Nike, Ralph Lauren, Diesel, and Clarks burned in brick kilns on the other. |

## Responses

| Page | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| [Existing solutions](existing-solutions.md) | Kloth Cares, Style Theory, and moreloop are working the problem in Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand, all at a fraction of the scale of Korea's fully government-run food-waste system. |
| [International organizations' response](international-response.md) | The UN Alliance for Sustainable Fashion, launched 2019 by eight UN agencies, coordinates policy and analysis on fashion sustainability — it doesn't fund or run collection/recycling programs on the ground. |
| [Household behavior lessons](household-behavior-lessons.md) | Korea's metered food-waste bins worked; California's food-waste law hasn't, because it never changed household behavior. Nothing like Korea's system exists yet for textiles anywhere in this wiki. |
