# Southeast Asia: Where Developed-Country Fashion Waste Lands

This is the core page for the team's chosen focus: what happens in Southeast Asia as a result of fast fashion and overconsumption elsewhere. Two distinct waste streams show up in the sources here, and they are **not the same problem** — one is imported secondhand clothing (post-consumer, arriving from donations/exports abroad), the other is factory offcuts from garments manufactured in the region for developed-country brands (pre-consumer). Both land on the same population; neither should be quoted as evidence for the other.

## Stream 1: imported secondhand clothing

- Malaysia imports an estimated **220,000 tonnes/year** of secondhand clothing, per UN Comtrade data (2015–2019) — the only Southeast Asian country in the global top four importers by weight, behind only Pakistan (640,000 tonnes/year) and India (330,000 tonnes/year), and roughly on par with Angola (190,000 tonnes/year). [Source](../sources/global-secondhand-clothing-trade-data.md)
- Indonesia has **banned secondhand clothing imports since 2015** specifically to protect its domestic textile industry. In a single raid in late March 2023, officials in West Java destroyed **more than 7,000 bundles** of illegally imported secondhand clothing, valued at **$5.3 billion**. [Source](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md)
- That ban has a real human cost on the Indonesian side of the trade: **more than 26 million people** are officially recorded as living below Indonesia's poverty line, the population the secondhand-clothing trade most directly serves. Seller Siti reported earning roughly **$536** from selling used garments every few weeks — *"If there are no clothes, that means no money."* [Source](../sources/indonesia-secondhand-clothing-crackdown.md)
- Malaysia is separately estimated to produce **almost 2,000 tonnes of textile waste a day** domestically — a figure about the country's own waste generation, not imports. [Source](../sources/sea-kloth-cares-circular-fashion.md)

## Stream 2: factory waste from garments made for developed-country brands

- Cambodia's garment industry generates **"no less than 90,000 tons of garment waste per year."** [Source](../sources/cambodia-fast-fashion-waste-burning.md)
- An investigation found garment offcuts and rejects made for **Nike, Ralph Lauren, Diesel, and Clarks** being burned in Cambodian brick kilns operating around the clock, reaching temperatures **"up to 650C."** [Source](../sources/cambodia-fast-fashion-waste-burning.md)
- Because most garments are polyester, *"the burning of this plastic exposes bonded workers to toxic fumes and microplastic fibres."* Workers in these arrangements are described as **"bonded"** and engaged in **"a form of modern-day slavery."** [Source](../sources/cambodia-fast-fashion-waste-burning.md)
- The wider claim behind this: **"For fashion consumed in the EU, 84.7% of the environmental impacts take place outside the EU, mostly in Asia."** [Source](../sources/cambodia-fast-fashion-waste-burning.md)
- The investigation's own conclusion is blunt: brands' **"claims of zero-waste and worker standards in supply chains... are no more than greenwashing"** without supply-chain transparency. [Source](../sources/cambodia-fast-fashion-waste-burning.md)

## What the sources don't say

No source in this wiki gives a per-country tonnage figure for Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam, or Cambodia's *imports* of secondhand clothing (only Malaysia has one, from UN Comtrade). And no source here quantifies what share of Malaysia's 220,000 imported tonnes ends up as waste versus resale — that's a real gap, not a number to estimate.
