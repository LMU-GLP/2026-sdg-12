# Global Fast-Fashion Footprint

The scale of the industry's environmental cost, compiled from figures published by several different organizations. These figures come from at least seven named sources on one compilation page, use different years and methodologies, and should not be averaged or combined into a single number. [Source](../sources/global-fast-fashion-footprint.md)

## Emissions and climate

- **2–8% of global carbon emissions** come from the fashion industry — World Resources Institute.
- Fashion's emissions could reach **26% of the global carbon budget by 2050** if current growth continues — Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017. (This is a projection, not the same measurement as the WRI figure above — don't state them as one range.)

## Water and pollution

- The industry uses **215 trillion litres of water annually** — described as equivalent to 86 million Olympic-sized swimming pools (Quantis, 2018), making it the **second-largest consumer of water globally**.
- **~20% of industrial wastewater pollution** globally is attributed to fashion/textile dyeing and treatment — World Bank, 2020.
- **500,000 tons of microfibers** are released into the ocean every year from washing synthetic textiles, equivalent to 50 billion plastic bottles — Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017.
- **9% of annual microfibre ocean pollution** originates from textiles — UNEP, 2021. (Note: this is a different figure from the 500,000-tons estimate above, from a different organization and year.)
- **60% of fashion materials** are made from synthetic (plastic) fibers — UNEP, 2019.

## Waste and disposal

- **85% of all textiles produced are dumped annually** — UNECE, 2018.
- *"One garbage truck full of clothes is burned or dumped in a landfill every second"* — Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017.
- **$460 billion in annual value loss** from clothing discarded while still wearable — UN source, 2025.
- **Less than 1% of material** used to produce clothing is recycled into new clothing — Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017.
- **Only 0.3%** of the fashion industry's material inputs come from recycled sources — Circularity Gap Report Textiles, 2024.

## Why this page can't give you one clean number

Depending which organization is cited, fashion is responsible for somewhere between 2% and 8% of current global emissions, and could reach 26% of the remaining carbon budget by 2050 — two different kinds of measurement from two different organizations, not two ends of one range. What's consistent across every figure on this page is the direction: enormous material throughput, and almost none of it recycled back into new clothing.
